I joined Green Feather Software (later FeatherPay) when it was an early-stage healthcare payments product, so the first job was learning who it was for and what would make them care.

I led customer and market research and used it to shape the product’s positioning and go-to-market approach. I then ran account-based marketing and email automation in Pardot, using A/B testing to learn which messages earned engagement, and created sales enablement and customer-facing content that supported the company’s first client wins.

The same work doubled as product-market-fit validation: what resonated with prospects fed back into positioning and roadmap recommendations.

## The company

FeatherPay was an Atlanta fintech startup built to make paying for healthcare simpler for both practices and patients, starting with dental, optometry, audiology and similar practices where patients cover much of the cost themselves.

According to DevPro Journal, the FeatherPay company was formed in 2021, found early traction with customers, and was acquired by iCoreConnect on January 1, 2024, when it became iCorePay, a fintech division of iCoreConnect. The article also describes the team’s early approach: testing a handful of ideas through outbound marketing to healthcare practices, then building around the messages about payments and the patient experience that drew the most interest.

## The customer case study

Green Feather’s sales team needed proof that the product worked, so I wrote and designed a customer case study. It tells the story of a large healthcare division, described only as a unit of a Fortune 500 company, that sold consumer medical devices online and by phone and was losing sales whenever patients couldn’t cover the out-of-pocket cost. Adding more payment options had only made checkout more complicated.

The case study explains how Feather Pay put those options into one simple screen and connected payments to the client’s order system, so their accounting team didn’t have to change anything. According to the case study, the payments led to a 7% increase in overall revenue in the first 30 days, and average order value on those orders was 16% higher than on orders that didn’t use Feather Pay. It also includes testimonials from a senior director and a telesales rep, and notes that the client began emphasizing affordability in its own marketing.

Those numbers describe the product’s impact for that customer, not the results of my campaigns. The case study is how I told that story to prospects: a clear problem, a simple solution, and results a buyer could picture.
